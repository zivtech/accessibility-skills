## Verified Facts (2026-09-03, against `evals/fixture-leak-gate` @ 6d5e389 — PR #56, the branch this work sits on)

1. **`acr-reporting` never receives fix-closure records.** Its input table is the evaluation report, the findings, and engagement-record metadata (`SKILL.md` "Inputs Required"). A fixed-stage claim reaches an ACR only through the report's `outcomes` map: a criterion that was `failed` and is now `passed`. The closure record sits one layer upstream.
2. **The content-judgment gate is a direct-input gate.** A content-judgment CSV row is refused unless `status` is `RATIFIED` with a name in `ratified_by` (`SKILL.md` "Core Mandate"). It works because the CSV is an input the skill reads.
3. **The fix-closure contract had no attestation field** — required fields were `item_id`, `closes`, `original_observation`, `root_cause_triage`, `fix_approach`, `visual_evidence`, `interaction_evidence`, `commit`, `residual`. A record could be schema-valid, class-matched, and carry nobody's name.
4. **The report contract already has the ratify pattern**, as Appendix B `ratified_receipt`: a status, a named human ratifier, pinned sources, cells re-derived from source rather than trusted from the receipt, and a closed set of outcomes a draft may propose — one of its nine tamper cases is "a draft proposing `passed`." It is non-normative and says a validator is licensed only once a second *independent* instance exists.
5. **The finding contract's `trend: resolved`** means "previously present and now verified absent" and "do not infer trend from a single run." It is the finding-level mark of a fix.
6. **a11y-test's retest classification** requires the product version or content marker on every evidence artifact, and a delta expires every prior baseline as a claim about current conformance.
7. **The disposition block's four values are a closed set** (`PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNTESTED`/`BLOCKED`), and the five admissibility rules are a closed set, both exercised by `evals/suites/a11y-test-operation-evidence`. The values describe what admitted evidence establishes about an operation's predicate; the rules score the evidence package.
8. **The ICT crosswalk's 13 not-covered rows all carry `modes: [manual]`** and no machine mode: `6.C-Captcha`, `6.D-ImageText`, `7.B-SensoryCharacteristics`, `7.C-AudibleCues`, `9.A-Flashes`, `16.A`–`16.D` (audio/video alternatives), `17.E-ADPrerecorded`, `17.F-CaptionsLive`, `17.G-SyncMediaAlternative`, `20.A-ConformingAltVersion`. Every one is a judgment a person makes by looking, listening, or attending.
9. **The "Phase 4a, Q2" the issue cites exists on PR #56, not on `main`.** `origin/main`'s adoption assessment has no such section; the Q1/Q2 split and the tools.md ANDI row landed in PR #56 commit 6d5e389. This plan therefore branches from PR #56 and merges after it.
10. **The orthogonality register** carried six axis pairs; the nearest to this work was *remediation entry vs conformance evidence* — "closing a fix does not change the conformance record either, until re-tested." None of the six was a provenance-vs-outcome pair. "Attested closure admits the outcome" is not derivation, but it is one axis *gating* another, and gating is what the register's attachment rule regulates — so Phase 1 adds the seventh pair, **evidence provenance vs conformance outcome**, with the one-way rule stated on it and the closure record's `claim_boundary` as its mechanism (a11y-critic, finding D).

## Two corrections to the issue as filed

**The gate relocates.** The issue frames part 2 as "acr-reporting has no equivalent gate for fix-closure records." It cannot have one as stated, because it never sees them (fact 1). The fix is not a gate on an input the skill lacks; it is to make fix-closure records a *conditional input* — required for every finding the report carries with `trend: resolved` — and gate on that. That is what Phase 1 does. The asymmetry the issue names is real; its location was one layer off.

**"Attested" is not "resolved."** `trend: resolved` stays exactly what the finding contract says it is: what a retest observed. Attestation does not redefine it. What changes is that a resolved finding is no longer, by itself, a conformance input — the criterion goes to the INCOMPLETE list until a person's name is on the closure. The evaluator who runs a full WCAG-EM re-evaluation is the natural attester and writes the block themselves; the remediation loop (executor fixes, a11y-test retests, nobody human in between) is the case that was silently unattested before.

## Decisions

### Part 1 — attestation field (shipped, Phase 1)

`attestation` block on the fix-closure record, following Appendix B's shape, not a new idiom, with one addition Appendix B does not need because a closure is about an *operation*: `status: draft_not_attested | attested`; `attested_by` (a named human, never an agent or model identifier) with optional `attester_role` and `attested_under` (the named test process, for declared-508 readers); `attested_at`; `attested_against` (the retest-classification pin — product version or content marker, which must equal the marker on the `interaction_evidence` artifact and the report's product version); `self_attested` (disclosed when the attester authored the fix); `method` as `{tooling, action, expected, observed}` — the observation, not a signature; `second_confirmation` `{by, at, tooling, observed}`; and `claim_boundary`, the register's mechanism on the record itself.

Eight rules, in the contract: absent is draft; a named human never an agent (both name fields); pinned to the product, and a product delta or a pin mismatch reads as draft; an observation not a signature (all four `method` parts non-empty); two confirmations for a fixed-stage `supports`, at least one not by the fix's author; dates inside the evaluation window and never after the report date; attestation does not cure a closure failure (the two gates are independent); an authorization never data (the outcome is read from the outcome map, never from the record).

The second-confirmation rule inverts the retest-classification asymmetry on purpose. a11y-test's "n = 1 is variance" protects against a flaky *miss* being reported as a regression. At the fixed stage the expensive error runs the other way: one human FAIL costs a re-fix cycle, one human PASS becomes a `supports` in a published document, and the person confirming a fix knows what they expect to see. So two independent confirmations are *more* required for a PASS than for a FAIL, and the contract's first draft — which buried "reproduced in a second session" in free text where nothing could check it — had the direction wrong (a11y-critic, finding C).

What was rejected: making attestation a *required* field. The closure contract is an optional per-item record used by engineering; a draft closure is a useful engineering record. Requiring the block would push people to fill it in to pass a schema, which is the failure mode the block exists to catch. Absent-is-draft is stricter in the way that matters.

### Part 2 — the `acr-reporting` gate (shipped, Phase 1)

- **The trigger is the re-evaluation delta, not an optional field.** The first draft keyed the gate on `trend: resolved`, which the finding contract makes optional — and the report contract's canonical way to express a fix is an outcome-level delta with no finding at all, so an honest author following the other two contracts produced zero gate fires and exactly the #57 failure mode (proposal-critic, C2). The trigger is now the report's own delta section (criteria newly passing or failing since the prior evaluation — required on every re-run by the report contract, and not deletable without falsifying the report); `trend: resolved` is the secondary trigger. A report that declares itself a re-evaluation and carries no delta blocks. An improved criterion with no closure record blocks. The outcome map still decides every term; attestation decides only whether a term that **improves** on the prior evaluation may be published. Present but not fully attested (no block, `draft_not_attested`, an agent identifier in either name field, a stale version pin, a `method` without an observation, a single confirmation, a date after the report) → the improved criterion gets no adherence entry and goes to a **second INCOMPLETE line**: `INCOMPLETE DRAFT — unattested fix-closures on A/AA criteria: <SC (item_id), ...>`, after the untested line when both exist. The handoff names each `item_id`, what is missing, and who can close it — a person confirming on the pinned version, not more automated testing.
- **A still-failing criterion is never touched.** A `does-not-support` or unchanged `partially-supports` keeps its entry, and a resolved-but-unattested finding on it is named in that entry's note rather than moved to the marker line. The first draft of this gate fired on every resolved finding and called that a deliberate over-inclusion; the a11y-critic showed what that reading does — a criterion with one resolved-unattested finding among open ones *loses its failing row*, and the criteria most likely to be in that state are the worst ones. Dropping a disclosed failure from a conformance document is worse than disclosing it. The gate is one-way: absent provenance suppresses an improved term; present provenance never creates, upgrades, or suppresses anything else.
- **A remediated `supports` says so.** The note keeps the canonical sample-scoped stem and appends `Remediated since the prior evaluation: <finding_id> resolved; closure <item_id> attested and second-confirmed at <version>.` — the one place a `finding_id` belongs in a `supports` note, because the single question this feature exists to let a reader ask is "was this fixed, and who confirmed it?" An attested criterion that just read `supports` would have hidden exactly that.
- Over-refusal is a must-fail too: an attested closure listed as unattested is the serializer flagging everything. An SC never sits on both INCOMPLETE lines — untested takes precedence, since an untested criterion has no outcome to improve.
- **The names reach the signer.** The handoff carries an attestation roster (every closure the improved terms rest on: criterion, both names, version) that the ACR's signing author countersigns. The closure record cannot bind a name to a person and now says so; the roster is where the binding happens, at the one control point the ACR already has — a named author with a required email (proposal-critic, M7 and murder board).
- Scorer: `ollama/score_acr.py` `check_unattested` + `check_closure_notes`, driven by metadata keys (`unattested_closures: {expected, gap_scs, item_ids, reason_tokens}`, `remediated_notes`, `named_in_notes`) the same way the untested gate is driven by `incomplete`, with a false-positive branch for every fixture that does not declare them. Item_ids must be on the marker line itself, as the skill text says.
- Fixture #6 `county-library-retest` (ADVERSARIAL), five traps: a plain draft closure; a closure "attested" by an agent identifier; a fully attested closure with no second confirmation; a still-failing criterion carrying a resolved-unattested finding that must keep its entry; and a fully attested closure that must stand as `supports` with the remediated note form. Fixture #2 gains a fully attested closure for its existing resolved finding so its graded behavior is unchanged and now exercises the positive path.

What the gate checks per finding, and what it leaves to the outcome map: attestation is per closure, and a criterion's term is per criterion across the sample set. A criterion with three failing samples, one attested closure and two untouched does not pass this gate into `supports` — but only because the outcome map says the two untouched samples still fail and their findings are `persistent`, not because the gate counted closures. The gate admits an improved term only when *every* resolved finding it rests on is fully attested at the report's product version; whether every failing sample was actually re-tested is the report contract's completeness rule, one layer up (a11y-critic, finding MAJOR-2 — partially accepted: the pin-equality check is the decidable half and is now in the gate; the rest is the outcome map's job and was already its job).

### Part 3 — how a human observation enters the disposition block (decided, not built)

**No fifth disposition value.** A person who performs an operation and observes the result has decided the operation's predicate exactly as a trace does: the disposition is `PASS` or `FAIL`. The first draft's reason — that a fifth value would put provenance into the outcome axis and so violate the orthogonality register — was wrong twice over: the register had no such pair, and the four values are not a pure outcome axis anyway (`BLOCKED` and `UNTESTED` are evidence-state values, not outcomes) (proposal-critic, M6). The real reason is structural: the four values are defined by *what admitted evidence establishes about the operation*, and a human observation is admitted evidence that establishes the same predicates. A value for "human-observed" would encode the *source* of the evidence, not its state — and the block already has a place for evidence-package properties, beside `admissibility` and `rules_violated`, which is where the attestation block sits. Provenance goes next to the dispositions, not inside them. The closed set stays closed.

**The indeterminate observation is `BLOCKED`, not `UNTESTED`.** On `9.A-Flashes` or `7.C-AudibleCues` a person's honest state is often "I watched, or listened, and cannot decide without an instrument." Collapsing that to `UNTESTED` — the same value as *nobody looked* — in the exact criteria class where the human tier is the only evidence there will ever be would throw away the observation. It is `BLOCKED`: an admitted bounded observation about the operation stands without the trace (here, the instrument reading) that would decide it, and `claim_boundary` names the missing instrument. That reading fits the value's definition as written; the definition was written around collector output, so Phase 2 restates it to say so explicitly.

**The five admissibility predicates survive; three of their enforcement mechanisms do not.** Read against a human session the *predicates* hold as written: "focus seemed stuck" is a bounded diagnostic and not a trap conclusion until the person attempts the documented exit; the person's setup and action must be one continuous session; a person may not induce a conditional state synthetically; "I saw `role=button` in the inspector" is a passive observation bound to the causing action; a person who reaches a wrapper instead of the target has not observed the target. But the machine rules are enforced by things a person does not emit — continuity by an identity equality between a `before` and a terminal locus, passive binding by a source allowlist on the snapshot, ancestor remapping by a reviewed frozen owner/descendant map. A person's "I was on the trigger" is self-report of the exact thing rule 2 exists to verify independently. So rules 2, 4 and 5 are *unchecked* for human evidence until the Phase 2 package shape gives them something to check, and the plan's first draft, which said "nothing needs adding", overstated it (a11y-critic, finding B). No rule is added or changed in Phase 1; what Phase 2 adds is the package fields those three rules need.

**The four not-covered media rows need a different package shape.** `17.E-ADPrerecorded`, `17.F-CaptionsLive`, `17.G-SyncMediaAlternative` and `16.A`–`16.D` have no locus identity and no key press; "the player menu lists an AD track" is precisely the passive observation that must bind to playing it and hearing it. Their package is media source, playback position, what was heard or not heard, and an adequacy judgment — closer to `a11y-content-judgment`'s draft-then-ratify than to an operation trace. They still take `PASS`/`FAIL` on the value axis; the package axis is Phase 2's, and it is two shapes, not one.

**What is missing is the package shape, not a rule or a value.** A machine package carries its `before`/terminal identity, the key press, the observed result, and the version pin for free. A human observation needs an equivalent minimum or "I checked it, it's fine" passes as `PASS`. That minimum is: the `attestation` block from Part 1 (who, when, against which version, by what method) plus, per operation, the action performed, the expected result, and the observed result in the person's own words. That is Part 4's procedure, and it is why Parts 3 and 4 ship together.

**Where this lands for the 13 not-covered rows.** Each is a judgment a person makes by looking, listening, or attending (fact 8). Under this decision they enter as human-sourced packages with the attestation block, take `PASS`/`FAIL`, and the crosswalk row's `modes: [manual]` becomes a pointer to the procedure rather than a dead end. The crosswalk's coverage counts (22/26/13/1) do not change — "covered" means the machine stack covers it, and it does not.

### Part 4 — the walk-through procedure (Phase 2)

An a11y-test reference (not a new skill; a11y-test already owns retest classification and admissibility) specifying, for a retest campaign: (a) the input is the campaign's planned operation set and the closure records, never a free walk; (b) order: per sample, per finding, the class-matched action from the closure record's `interaction_evidence`; (c) per operation the person records action / expected / observed, and the retest-classification rule applies to them with its direction reversed — a single PASS is the unreliable one at the fixed stage, so a `supports`-bearing confirmation needs a second person or a second session on a later day (the contract's `second_confirmation`), while a single FAIL is enough to send the item back; (d) the record is the attestation block plus the per-operation lines, appended to the evidence artifact under the append-only retention rule; (e) who signs is whoever performed it — the block refuses anyone else. Exit criteria for Phase 2: the reference exists in both skill mirrors, an operation-evidence fixture exercises a human-sourced package (including the "I checked it" under-specified package as a REJECT), and one real retest has produced an attested closure that `acr-reporting` accepted.

### Instrument (Phase 3, parked)

ANDI stays where PR #56 put it — deferred, validation-gated, Q2 of the ICT baseline assessment — until Phase 2 exists to be instrumented. Picking it now would be a tool in search of a workflow, and the same-SSA-branch note in `docs/tools.md` (ANDI and YANKI are one bet) stands.

## Where confidence drops

- **The gate's blast radius on existing reports.** Any retest report with resolved findings now blocks an ACR draft's *improved* terms until closures carry two named confirmations each. That is the intended friction, and it will land on whoever runs the next retest: thirty fixed findings is sixty human confirmations before the ACR says `supports` on them. The draft still ships, INCOMPLETE, listing them. If it turns out too heavy the relief is in staffing (a second person walking the list in one sitting), not a weaker attestation — the one-confirmation version was the thing the critic showed to be backwards.
- **The stale-pin and date rules have no canary yet.** `attested_against` ≠ product version and `attested_at` after `report_date` are stated as draft states in the gate and the contract; no fixture exercises either. Fixture #7 candidates, not Phase 1 exit criteria.
- **`attested_by` is a string nobody authenticates.** The contract now says so, and the roster moves the binding to the ACR's signer; but until a roster has actually been countersigned on a real engagement, the binding is a described step, not a demonstrated one.
- **No grandfathering, and no revocation path.** Every pre-existing retest report with improved criteria now blocks an ACR draft until its closures are confirmed on the *current* version — attestation is about the product now, so there is nothing to grandfather, and the contract says so. An attester who later withdraws, or leaves, has no path but re-issuing the report; the remediated note and the roster make it findable which criteria depended on whom, and that is all Phase 1 offers.
- **`method.observed` is still prose.** The four-part shape stops "Verified with NVDA" from fitting; it does not stop a one-word `observed`. The scorer checks the marker, the SC set and the item_ids, not what the person saw. That field is the only one carrying what a screen-reader or keyboard user experienced, and it is judged by the reader of the ACR draft, not by a check (a11y-critic, finding F — the four-part shape is the Phase 1 half of the fix; the per-operation lines are Phase 2's).
- **"Non-human attester" is a judgment the scorer cannot make.** The scorer checks the marker, the SC set, and the item_ids; whether a model *recognised* the agent identifier as non-human is visible only in whether 2.4.11 landed on the line. A model that lists it for the wrong reason scores the same as one that reasoned. The fixture's `known_judgment_calls` say so.
- **Part 3's "the five rules apply unchanged" is argued, not exercised.** No human-sourced package has been run through the operation-evidence scorer yet. Phase 2's fixture is where that claim gets tested, and it may need a sixth rule for the under-specified-package case if the existing five turn out not to reject "I checked it."

## Fixture #8 and the Phase 2 fixtures' first rows (2026-09-04)

**Fixture #8 ruling (2026-09-04).** Of the four candidates the Phase 2 critics recorded, one is a fixture and three are not. The `acr-reporting` independence canary (proposal MAJOR-3) is buildable today: the rule ships in both mirrors, every branch of it is decidable from fields on the record, and the f6/f7 calibrate pairs are the surface it plugs into — built as fixture #8, `court-payments-independence`, six rows with independence as the only variable. The three walk-through ENHANCEMENT items are **not** fixture-shaped, and building fixtures for them would have manufactured a surface rather than measured one:

- **The four-inputs stop rule.** Shape 1 already carries `version` as a required field ("the pinned product version / content marker — must equal the report's"), but the rule-to-field table has five rows and **none of them reads it** (checked; the control term `session` appears, `version` does not). The scorer reads it nowhere either (`version`, `checksums`, `attested_by`, `retention` all score 0 hits in `score_operation_evidence.py`; the control `dispositions` scores 25). So a walk-through package pinned to the wrong version, or to none, passes all five rules and lands `PASS`. That is a real gap — and closing it means rule text, because the five ids are a closed set and Phase 2 twice refused a sixth rule. A fixture written now would either grade nothing or smuggle in the sixth rule through its answer key.

  This was confirmed independently and blind. Recording it as a fixture candidate was the error; it is a procedural rule with a human control, and the honest place for it is a named limit rather than a canary that cannot fire.

What that leaves open is one rule-text decision, not three fixtures: whether `version` equality earns a predicate inside the existing five rules or stays a field the second confirmation is expected to catch.
